# Explain PromiseKeeper to a small-business owner

## A 30-second explanation

“Before you chase an unpaid invoice, you need to know what was invoiced, what payment is actually recorded and what the customer promised. PromiseKeeper puts those records together and suggests the next responsible step: follow up, wait, verify a payment or close the case. You can open the source behind the recommendation, review the exact draft and keep the final decision. It keeps a history so you can pick the case up later without starting over.”

## Show the value in five minutes

1. **Start with their problem:** “When a customer says ‘I already paid’, how do you decide whether to send a reminder?” Listen for the records they check and the time they spend. Do not promise savings you have not measured.
2. **Show Northstar:** invoice 120,000, verified payment 40,000, missed80,000 promise. Show the 80,000 follow-up and click its original source. Explain that the amount comes from the records.
3. **Show Meridian:** the 50,000 promise has not expired. The app says WAIT. Explain why a reminder today could be premature.
4. **Show Cedar:** the customer claims payment, but the matching verified entry is missing. The app says VERIFY_PAYMENT. Apply the fictional settlement update, investigate again, then show that closure still needs the owner's confirmation.
5. **Give them control:** edit a supported greeting, save and approve that exact version, then show its history. Say plainly: “Approval here records a decision; this version does not send messages.”

## Why someone might pay

The value to test is less time reconstructing a case, fewer premature reminders and a clear explanation of who decided what. The product should earn its price by making that work measurably easier. Ask the owner to complete the same fictional review manually and with the app; compare time, correctness and confidence. Do not claim the 30% time-saving study target has been achieved before measuring it.

Ask: “Would this fit the way you manage overdue invoices? Which record would you need connected first? If a secure pilot proved useful on your workflow, would you consider paying, and what would make it worth that price?” Record their answer without implying a purchase commitment. Pricing and willingness to pay have not been validated.

## Honest answers to common objections

| Owner asks | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| “Will it chase my customers automatically?” | “You review the next step. The current prototype sends no messages.” |
| “Does it know whether the money reached my bank?” | “It uses designated verified ledger records. A customer message alone is a claim; missing or conflicting evidence leads to verification.” |
| “Will it connect to my accounting or WhatsApp?” | “This release uses supplied fictional cases. Those connectors are future work, with access and security designed before real data is used.” |
| “Can I use my customer list today?” | “This is a local demonstration for fictional records. Real-data ingestion, authentication and production controls are not implemented.” |
| “Can the AI make a mistake?” | “The suggestion is tied to the source, and uncertain or failed work is shown explicitly. You decide what to do. The frozen LIVE evaluation is recorded with its provider gap; owner usability still needs validation before we claim release readiness.” |
| “Why should I buy now?” | “Try the demonstration and judge the workflow. A paid production offer needs validated value, reliable LIVE behavior and the controls your business needs; this prototype does not establish those yet.” |

## A suitable close today

“Try these three fictional cases and tell me whether the reasoning is clear and whether you stay in control. If this solves a real problem for you, we can use that feedback to define a secure pilot with clear success measures.”

This is a demonstration and discovery script, not a claim that the current prototype is ready for sale or that a customer has accepted it. No automated outreach, billing, deployment or recruitment is part of this work.
